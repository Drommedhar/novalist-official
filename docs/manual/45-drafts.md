# Drafts

A **draft** is one version of a book's manuscript: its own chapters, its own scenes, its own prose. Every book has at least one. Create more when you want a rewrite kept separate from the original — a zero draft, the cut you sent to beta readers, the one going to an agent.

The **Drafts** view is where they are managed. Open it from **Write** in the mode rail, under **Drafting**, or from the command palette. The draft selector in the toolbar stays the fastest way to *switch* between them; this view is for everything else.

## The list

Every draft of the open book, one row each, in your order:

- **Name** — click it and type. A draft could only be named at the moment it was created before; now it can be renamed whenever what it is for becomes clearer.
- **What this draft is for** — a line of your own: "agent submission", "pre-beta cut", "the one where Mira lives". Optional, and the reason a list of four drafts is readable a year later.
- **Counts** — how many chapters and scenes the draft holds, read from the draft itself, so a nearly-empty experiment is obvious without opening it.
- **You are here** marks the draft you are working in. Any other row has an **Open** button that switches to it.
- **Duplicate** copies the whole draft under a new name. The copy remembers which draft it came from, which is what lets [Compare drafts](03-projects-and-books.md#working-with-drafts) show you what the rewrite actually changed.
- **Delete** removes a draft and everything written in it, after a confirmation. A book always keeps at least one.

**Drag a row by its handle** to reorder the list. The order is yours — creation order is rarely the order you think in — and it is the order the toolbar's draft selector uses too.

## Sending chapters and scenes to another draft

Under the list is **Send content to another draft**. Pick the draft to read **From**, the draft to send **To**, and tick what should go:

- **Tick a chapter** to send it whole, with every scene under it.
- **Tick single scenes** to send only those. The chapter they belong to is created in the target draft if it is not there already — a scene has to live somewhere.

You can send from a draft you are *not* currently in; the view reads it straight from its folder.

**The right-hand column is the target draft as this transfer would leave it** — everything already in it, plus what is arriving, with a mark on every row that changes: **new** for content the target does not have yet, **rewritten** for a scene it already has whose prose this would replace. Rows that nothing touches stay dim. Nothing is written until you press a button, so it is safe to tick things and look.

Two buttons finish the job:

- **Copy** leaves the source draft exactly as it was. This is the one to reach for.
- **Move** removes what you sent from the source afterwards. A chapter left with nothing in it goes with them; a chapter that still holds scenes you did not send stays. Moving always asks first.

### What happens when the target already has it

Drafts are usually clones of one another, so the same scene commonly exists in both under the same identity. Sending it again **rewrites the prose of the copy that is already there** rather than leaving two of them side by side — which is what you want when the point was to bring the rewritten chapter across, and it keeps the two drafts comparable afterwards.

The view says so before it happens: the line above the buttons counts how many of the selected scenes the target already has, and copying over them asks for confirmation.

A scene that crosses keeps its identity, so a scene sent to another draft and then compared against its origin still reads as one scene changed, not one added and one removed.

## What drafts do not separate

Entities, images, plotlines, templates and the calendar are per **book**, not per draft. All drafts of a book share them. Only the chapter tree and the prose are a draft's own.
