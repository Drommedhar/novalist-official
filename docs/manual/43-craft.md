# Craft reference

Novalist's word lists were machine-readable analysis stems shown to nobody: the app could count filter words and could not help you find a better one. A blank page had nothing behind it, and a scene that needed a body-language beat sent you to a browser, which is where writing sessions go to die.

Open a scene and find **Craft** in the Context tab of the inspector. Three tabs.

## Write

Something to write when the page is blank. **Another** gives the next one; the picker holds it to a kind:

- **A scene** — situations with a want and an obstacle already in them.
- **A character** — questions about somebody you are building.
- **A place** — questions about somewhere.
- **When stuck** — not prompts so much as moves. Skip the part you are dreading. Let the character say the thing. Cut the first three paragraphs.

## Describe

A thesaurus of **specifics**, not synonyms. Look up a feeling, a place or a sense and you get concrete, observable details to steal from.

The entries never contain an adjective for the thing itself. "She was frightened" is the sentence this exists to replace, so the fear entry offers *a swallow that will not go down*, *checking the exit without meaning to*, *the cold that starts at the wrists*.

Search reads the entry names **and** their details, so typing a detail you half-remember finds the entry it belongs to.

## Read

Short pieces on the craft, meant to be read while a scene is open rather than saved for later. Each is about something Novalist already models — point of view, stakes, scene shape, revision order — so reading one and using the app are the same motion.

These are in English for now, and the panel says so. The prompts and the thesaurus are in every bundled language.
