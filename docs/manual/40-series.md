# Series

The Series view is the project above the book. Everything else in Novalist reads the book you have open; this one reads all of them.

Open it from **Series** in the **Plan** group of the activity bar.

## Books

Every book in the project with its size and shape: chapters, scenes, words, and how many scenes have a [stage](10-manuscript.md#scene-stages) set. Each row also carries an **author** field, for an anthology whose volumes are by different people: what you type here prints under that volume's heading when the book is part of a [box set](20-export.md). Leave it empty and the book goes out under the project's author, which is what every book of a series wants. A project with one book says so — a series view starts to mean something once there are two.

## Shared entries across the books

A row per **World Bible** entry, with a cell per book, filled where the entry appears. A gap in the middle of a row is a thread that was dropped, which is very hard to see any other way.

Appearance means either of the two things Novalist treats as authoritative:

- the entry is in a scene's [cast](04-chapters-and-scenes.md), or
- the entry is `@`-mentioned in the prose and you confirmed the mention.

Guessing from names is deliberately not included: a name that happens to appear is not the same as a character being in the scene, and a series view built on guesses is worse than none.

Rows are sorted by how many books the entry reaches, so anything appearing in one book of three sits at the bottom — where a walk-on and an abandoned thread look the same, and only you can tell which it is.

Entries that belong to a single book are not listed. They cannot appear in another book, so the row would be a row of one every time. See [Codex](06-codex.md) for the difference between a book's own entries and the shared World Bible.

## What it costs

Reading the series means opening each book in turn, because a book's scene files hang off its own folder and there is no read-only way in. On a large multi-book project that takes a moment, and Novalist puts you back in the book you were in when it finishes. The view loads when you open it rather than staying live.
